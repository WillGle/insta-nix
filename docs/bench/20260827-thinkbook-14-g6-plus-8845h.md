# ThinkBook 14 G6+ AHP benchmark record

- Date: 2026-08-27
- Host: `think14gryzen`
- Hardware: Lenovo ThinkBook 14 G6+ AHP (`21LF`), AMD Ryzen 7 8845H with Radeon 780M
Scope: standard Linux benchmarks, developer builds, thermal/clock characterization, CPDA inspection, and the prior clock-cap diagnosis.

This is a record of the measurements actually obtained. A `PASS` means that the
tool completed; `INTERRUPTED` means that the run was stopped before its planned
duration; `FAIL` records a tool/configuration failure. No Cinebench score is
invented from the stress-test clocks.

## Frequency-cap diagnosis and post-cap verification

Before the GUI change, the live `amd-pstate` policy showed:

```text
scaling_max_freq=3801000
cpuinfo_max_freq=5137904
amd_pstate_max_freq=5137904
governor=performance
EPP=performance
boost=1
```

The 3.801 GHz limit was not caused by the governor. Journal evidence showed
`cpupower-gui` applying its saved login profile to CPUs 0–15 with a maximum of
`3801.0 MHz`. The NixOS configuration enables that service at
`hosts/think14gryzen/system.nix:94`.

After the user changed the GUI profile, all 16 policies were verified with:

```text
minimum=1100980 kHz
maximum=5137904 kHz
governor=performance
EPP=performance
```

`cpupower frequency-info` then reported a 1.10–5.14 GHz policy with boost
active. The later OC characterization therefore ran without the old 3.8 GHz
policy cap.

### Managed OC profile curve sweep

The three PPD-visible profiles managed by `ryzenadj-profile` were compared on AC
power with the same `matrixprod` workload. The local `sustained-build` overlay
was also measured; it uses the PPD `performance` base with lower Ryzenadj power
and temperature limits. Each profile had a 60-second one-core phase and a
480-second all-core phase, sampled every two seconds. The runner did not use
forced clocks, voltage, or curve-optimizer settings, stopped at 98 °C, and
restored the profile active at the start of each run.

| Profile | One-core peak | All-core mean | All-core peak | Peak Tctl | Peak APU PPT | Throttle delta | Stress result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `power-saver` | 3.771 GHz | 1.867 GHz | 3.765 GHz | 42.6 °C | 10.01 W | 0 | PASS |
| `balanced` | 4.816 GHz | 3.302 GHz | 4.841 GHz | 58.6 °C | 23.46 W | 0 | PASS |
| `sustained-build` | 4.816 GHz | 4.290 GHz | 4.840 GHz | 80.1 °C | 53.77 W | 0 | PASS |
| `performance` | 4.816 GHz | 4.506 GHz | 4.816 GHz | 96.6 °C | 62.63 W | 0 | PASS, thermal-near-limit |

`power-saver` intentionally exposes a roughly 3.801 GHz policy maximum;
`balanced`, `sustained-build`, and `performance` expose the full 5.138 GHz
policy maximum. This is profile behavior, not the former `cpupower-gui` cap.
`sustained-build` kept the all-core phase at 80.1 °C and 53.77 W peak APU PPT.
Its 4.290 GHz all-core mean was 29.91% above `balanced` and 4.79% below
`performance`; it was 16.6 °C cooler than `performance` with 8.86 W less peak
APU PPT. `performance` raises all-core effective frequency by about 36.5% over
`balanced`, but reaches the thermal-limit region. All eight phases passed
`stress-ng` verification with zero failed stressors.

### Power and sensor limits

The Waybar `APU (PPT)` field reads the `amdgpu` hwmon `power1_input`/
`power1_average` nodes. It is recorded in the CSV as `gpu_ppt_w`; it is not a
CPU-only watt reading. The current performance profile also describes
`48 W STAPM / 60 W slow / 64 W fast / 98 °C Tctl`, which explains seeing a
60-W PPT value in the interface.

`ryzenadj` was present, but the benchmark process could not access the SMU:

```text
Unable to get os_access Obj, check permission
Unable to init ryzenadj
```

No usable CPU package/RAPL watt reading or thermal-throttle counter was
available. The CSV therefore leaves `cpu_ppt_w` and the throttle counter as
`NA`. The reported `fan1=0 RPM` was not treated as a reliable fan measurement.

Waybar may show `100` for CPU utilization independently from temperature, and
its temperature display rounds to whole degrees. The telemetry captured a
97.1 °C Tctl maximum, not a sampled 100 °C, but stopping when Waybar appeared
to remain at 100 °C was the correct safety decision.

## Overall interpretation

- The post-GUI run removed the accidental 3.8 GHz limit.
- Single-core boost reached about 4.81 GHz.
- All-core performance held around 4.51 GHz for 23m43s with no large clock
  collapse.
- The limiting factor was thermal headroom: Tctl stayed around 96–97 °C.
- APU PPT was around 60 W, with a sampled maximum of 65.34 W; CPU-only watts
  remain unavailable.
- The all-core run is useful evidence of sustained behavior, but not a
  finished 30-minute stability pass.
- The standard Linux results and developer builds passed, but they were
  recorded separately from the post-cap OC characterization and should not be
  treated as a single controlled A/B experiment.
